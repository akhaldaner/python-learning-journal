# Philosophy

> Architect, not user. Build understanding in layers, not in a hurry.

---

## The core idea

An engineer is not someone who knows a language. An engineer is someone who understands the machine well enough to build with it — and honest enough to know what they do not yet understand.

This project treats learning as engineering: measurable, reproducible, verifiable. Reading is not learning. Watching is not learning. Only **reconstruction from memory, done by hand, and explained in writing to the mentor** counts.

## The T-shaped principle

Two axes, one person:

- **Deep vertical** — backend engineering: Python, SQL, APIs, Linux, system design.
- **Wide horizontal** — the systems layer underneath: memory model, C++, CUDA, linear algebra.

Both are built in parallel, over years, until they meet at the level where high-level services touch physical hardware.

## Order matters

Temptation says: start with GPU programming now — it is exciting. Discipline says: **become a backend engineer first**, then descend into systems.

- **Backend first** — that is where the market is, and where the resources for the deeper journey come from.
- **C++ second** — once working as an engineer, learn how memory and CPU really behave.
- **CUDA and AI infrastructure last** — the hardest layer, approached from stability rather than survival.

Mixing these layers too early is how students stall for years. Order is not a preference — it is strategy.

## Balance, not sprinting

Every subject moves every week. Mathematics feeds Computer Science. Computer Science explains what happens under Python. Linux shows the machine Python runs on. English opens access to everything else.

A week of "only Python" is not progress — it is drift. A skipped subject for two days is a signal to review the schedule, not to double the next session.

## Repetition beats volume

Knowledge that cannot be recalled is knowledge that does not exist.

Every topic passes through **clean repetition** — reconstructing the answer from memory, without looking — and **a written explanation to the mentor** — describing the mechanism in your own words — before it can be called *mastered*. This is slower at first, and much faster over years.

Spaced review over fixed intervals is not an optimization. It is the only thing that makes a second brain work.

## Small steps, not heroism

The plan fails the day it depends on heroism. Compensating a missed block at 2 a.m. breaks tomorrow. Cutting sleep to fit more study reduces tomorrow's capacity.

If a block is missed — it is marked and rescheduled, not absorbed at the cost of rest. Rest, movement, and sleep are part of the plan, not obstacles to it.

## Documents as engineering artifacts

Notes are not an archive. They are a second brain — and they must be maintained like code: structured, linked, valid, and honest about what is and is not yet known.

A note that does not connect to a knowledge map does not exist. A status *mastered* without an artifact is a lie to the future self. The hygiene of the document is part of the discipline of the engineer.

## What this is not

- Not a race.
- Not a shortcut.
- Not a template for "learn to code in 90 days".
- Not a claim that everyone should do this.

It is one experiment, run carefully, with all cards on the table.
